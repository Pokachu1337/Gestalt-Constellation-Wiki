Rarely called Fae or Fey, Honored Friends or Honored Neighbors are those that stem from a world other than our own, often called the Otherside or the Faerie, a plane alive in and of itself with its own twisted sense of humor. To call one "Fae" to its face is considered deeply discourteous by many, reducing an ancient people to a name given by those who never understood them. The Faerie is said to grow around an immense World Tree whose roots reach into [[Kreis]] itself. Wherever one of those roots brushes the mortal world, life flourishes. Great forests, ancient groves, crystal rivers, and beasts of impossible beauty are all said to have sprung from those hidden roots. Every leaf, every feather, and every stream remembers the Otherside, if only faintly. The Honored Neighbors live by laws unlike those of mortals. They care little for good or evil as we understand it, but promises are sacred, hospitality is remembered, and every gift demands another in return. A traveler shown kindness may find their family blessed for generations. Another who mocks an old custom or breaks their word may vanish without a trace, never to be spoken of again. To them, both outcomes are equally fair. Many stories tell of gentle strangers who healed the sick, blessed barren fields, or guided lost children safely home. Just as many tell of laughing crones who stole names, lured wanderers into the woods, or devoured those foolish enough to ignore old warnings. Neither tale is false. The Honored Neighbors simply act according to rules older than mankind, and rarely concern themselves with whether mortals find those rules kind. For this reason, [[Witches]] have long served as the bridge between the worlds. Many Stations kept by witches concern old pacts, forgotten paths, and the careful tending of the relationship between mortals and the Honored Neighbors. They know the proper greetings, the proper gifts, and when it is wiser to leave a bowl of milk on the doorstep than to ask who knocked in the night. Those who remember the old customs often say there is no safer friend than an Honored Neighbor who owes you a favor, and no greater fool than one who believes themselves clever enough to owe one in return.
