Since ancient times, many an obscure faith has held it that unmarried soldiers who die on the battlefield cannot pass on to the afterlife. Thus, their spirits linger and fester, and invite spiritual decay over many centuries. This, some say, is where the first undead came from; those who die without the blessing of holy matrimony, or at the very least those who die with such regrets as strong as a life without finding love. Nobody knows who they are, or how large their numbers may be. All that is known is that in recent years, abandoned battlefields and war-stricken ruins have been blessed, sanctified and cleansed without any organisation, church or order coming forward and claiming it to be their work -- yet sparse witnesses claim this to be the work of the Black-Veils. Known also as the Blackveil Brides, it is said that they are dark-clad, often gown-wearing women who purge cursed and unhallowed grounds, abandoned battlefields, of their death energies with a number of rites and cleansing rituals such as spreading salt, reciting prayers and more -- but their namesake comes from their symbolic rite of marrying the war-dead in large numbers at once. The logic goes that because they are dead, such marriages are instantly annulled, and so the Bride can move on to the next, and so on. Of course, nobody actually knows if they truly exist, or are simply a tale. They may not even be of this world; some theorize they are personifications of mourning as a concept itself. Whatever the case is, it is at least true that despite the devastating conflicts of today, the actual numbers of undead are far smaller than they ought to be. And, on occasion, bouquets of dark flowers have been found on such battlefields.
